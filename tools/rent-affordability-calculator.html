Rent Affordability Calculator

Short answer

Three common affordability tests, side by side. Example: $6,000 gross monthly income gives a maximum of $1,800 at the 30% rule ($6,000 × 30%), $2,400 at 2.5x ($6,000 ÷ 2.5), and $2,000 at 3x ($6,000 ÷ 3). A rent of $2,100 would be 35% of that income — above the 30% rule and above the 3x limit, though within 2.5x.

Affordability rules are rules of thumb, not laws. The 30% rule says housing should cost no more than 30% of gross income. The income-multiple rules invert the landlord tests: divide income by 2.5 or 3 to get a maximum rent. Seeing all three together shows why two renters with the same income can get different answers from different rules.

How much rent can you afford?

Formula

30% rule: maximum rent = gross monthly income × 30%. 2.5x: maximum rent = gross monthly income ÷ 2.5. 3x: maximum rent = gross monthly income ÷ 3. Rent-to-income ratio: rent ÷ gross monthly income × 100%.

Worked example

Gross monthly income $6,000. 30% rule: $6,000 × 30% = $1,800. 2.5x: $6,000 ÷ 2.5 = $2,400. 3x: $6,000 ÷ 3 = $2,000. Rent check for $2,100: $2,100 ÷ $6,000 = 35% — above the 30% rule, above the 3x maximum of $2,000, but within the 2.5x maximum of $2,400. Different rules, different answers: that is why all three are shown.

Limitations

  • These are rules of thumb, not legal limits and not a lender or landlord decision. A landlord may apply a different test, and a renter's other debts and costs matter too.
  • Gross-income tests can overstate what is comfortable to pay; the optional net-income 30% figure is the stricter cash view.
  • This tool covers renting only. It does not calculate mortgage affordability, home-loan qualification, or buying costs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 30% rule?

Housing costs should be no more than 30% of gross household income. Above 30% a household is often described as cost-burdened in housing guidance.

Why do 2.5x and 3x give different maxima?

They are different landlord screening multiples inverted: dividing income by a smaller multiple (2.5) gives a larger maximum rent than dividing by 3.

Gross or net income — which should I use?

Landlord tests normally use gross income. For your own budgeting, the net-income figure is the more honest cash test, because tax and deductions are already gone.
