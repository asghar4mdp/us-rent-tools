Lease Renewal Comparator

Short answer

Staying costs renewal rent × months. Moving costs new rent × months + moving costs + fees. Moving breaks even after one-time costs ÷ monthly saving, once the lower rent has covered the cost of moving. Example over 12 months: staying at $2,200 = $26,400; moving to $1,900 with $1,200 of one-time costs = $24,000, breaking even after 4.0 months.

Compare staying vs. moving

Formulas

  • Total cost of staying = renewal rent × months.
  • Total cost of moving = new-place rent × months + moving costs + fees.
  • Monthly difference = renewal rent − new-place rent. A positive figure means staying costs more per month before one-time costs.
  • Break-even months = (moving costs + fees) ÷ (renewal rent − new-place rent), when the renewal rent is higher than the new-place rent. After that many months, the rent saving from moving has covered the one-time costs.

Worked example

Current rent $1,800, renewal rent $1,950, new place $1,850, moving costs $800, fees $100, compared over 12 months.

  • Staying: $1,950 × 12 = $23,400.
  • Moving: $1,850 × 12 + $800 + $100 = $23,100 — $300 cheaper over 12 months.
  • Monthly difference: $1,950 − $1,850 = $100 per month.
  • Break-even: ($800 + $100) ÷ $100 = 9 months.

Limitations

  • This is a rent-and-one-time-cost comparison only. It does not include deposits (often refundable), utilities, commuting, or quality-of-life differences between the two places.
  • It assumes both rents stay flat over the comparison period. Real leases may include increases, concessions, or fee changes.
  • It makes no claim about whether a renewal increase is lawful — that depends on your lease and on state and city rules. Check the official source for your city and state, and the Rent Cap calculator where a verified cap exists.

Frequently asked questions

How is the staying total calculated?

Renewal rent multiplied by the number of months compared.

How is the moving total calculated?

New rent multiplied by the number of months, plus one-time moving costs and fees.

What is the break-even point?

The number of months after which the lower monthly rent has covered the one-time moving costs: one-time costs divided by the monthly saving.
