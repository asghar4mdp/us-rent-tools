Rent or Sell Calculator

Short answer

Example: on the worked-example figures — a $400,000 home with a $250,000 mortgage, 6% selling costs, $2,400 monthly rent, and a 5-year horizon — renting it out ends $75,966.88 ahead of selling now. Monthly cash flow starts slightly negative, at -$152.40 in year 1, and turns positive by year 5, at $110.87 a month.

Deciding whether to sell your home now or rent it out comes down to comparing two totals: the net proceeds from selling today, and everything you would end up with if you rented it out for a few years and sold later — the later sale proceeds plus the rental cash flow along the way. This tool runs that comparison on your own figures, so you can see which path leaves more money over the horizon you choose. If you are choosing between two rentals instead, use the Rent Compare Calculator — it compares two rentals against each other, while this tool compares selling your own home with renting it out. For the full explanation, see Rent or Sell Your Home Explained.

Should you sell now or rent it out?

The form starts with the worked-example figures — replace them with your own figures before calculating.

Formula

Net proceeds if sold now: home value × (1 − selling costs) − mortgage balance. Year-1 monthly cash flow: rent × (1 − vacancy) × (1 − management) − (taxes + insurance + maintenance) ÷ 12 − HOA − mortgage payment. Total net rental cash flow over the horizon: the sum over each year of 12 × rent × (1 + rent growth)year × (1 − vacancy) × (1 − management), minus the horizon in years × (taxes + insurance + maintenance + 12 × HOA), minus 12 × mortgage payment × horizon. Home value at the horizon: home value × (1 + appreciation)horizon. Mortgage balance at the horizon: mortgage balance × (1 + i)n − mortgage payment × ((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i, where i is the monthly mortgage rate and n is the horizon in months (if the rate is 0%, the balance is the starting balance minus the payments made). Net proceeds if sold at the horizon: home value at the horizon × (1 − selling costs) − mortgage balance at the horizon. Rent-path total: net proceeds if sold at the horizon + total net rental cash flow. Difference: rent-path total − net proceeds if sold now.

Worked example

Home value $400,000; mortgage balance $250,000; selling costs 6% (example); monthly rent $2,400; mortgage payment $1,500 a month; mortgage rate 6% a year; property taxes $4,800 a year; insurance $1,800 a year; maintenance $2,400 a year; HOA $0; vacancy 5% (example); management 8% (example); horizon 5 years; appreciation 3% a year (example); rent growth 3% a year (example).

Net proceeds if sold now: $400,000 × 0.94 − $250,000 = $126,000.00. Year-1 monthly cash flow: $2,400 × 0.95 × 0.92 − $750 − $0 − $1,500 = -$152.40. Year-5 monthly cash flow: $110.87. Net rent after vacancy and management over 5 years: $133,637.32. Total net rental cash flow over 5 years: -$1,362.68. Home value after 5 years: $463,709.63. Mortgage balance after 5 years: $232,557.49 (principal paid $17,442.51). Net proceeds if sold after 5 years: $203,329.56. Rent-path total: $201,966.88. Difference: renting out ends $75,966.88 ahead. Breakdown: appreciation after selling costs $59,887.05 + principal paid $17,442.51 + net cash flow -$1,362.68 = $75,966.88.

Limitations

  • The assumptions drive the result. Selling costs, rents, vacancy, management, appreciation, and rent growth are all entered by you — change any of them and the verdict can change, so test a range of figures rather than trusting one run.
  • Selling costs and rents vary by market. The 6%, 5%, 8%, 3%, and 3% figures in the worked example are labelled example values only, not claims about any market.
  • No tax is computed. Selling can involve capital gains rules such as the Section 121 exclusion, and renting can involve depreciation recapture — talk to a tax professional before deciding.
  • The model is simple: it does not reinvest the sale proceeds or the rental cash flow, and it does not inflate taxes, insurance, maintenance, or HOA costs over the horizon.
  • This is a money comparison only. It says nothing about being a landlord, tenant risk, repairs, local rental rules, or how much your time is worth — and it is not financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

Should I sell my home or rent it out?

It depends on your figures. Enter your home value, mortgage, selling costs, rent, and costs, and compare the net proceeds from selling now with the total from renting it out and selling later. The worked example favours renting, but different figures can reverse the answer.

Why is the monthly cash flow negative in the worked example?

In the example, rent of $2,400 a month falls to $2,097.60 after 5% vacancy and 8% management, while taxes, insurance, and maintenance add $750 a month and the mortgage payment is $1,500 — a total of $2,250 a month against $2,097.60 of rent, leaving a shortfall of $152.40 in year 1. Rent growth turns it positive by year 5, at $110.87 a month.

Does this calculator include taxes?

No. It compares money in and money out only and computes no tax. Selling can involve capital gains rules such as the Section 121 exclusion, and renting can involve depreciation recapture — talk to a tax professional before deciding.

What selling costs should I enter?

Enter the percentage of the sale price you expect to pay to sell, including agent commission and closing costs. The worked example uses 6% only as a labelled example figure — enter your own estimate for your market and sale.

What happens if I enter a 0% mortgage rate?

The calculator handles it: with no interest, the mortgage balance falls by the monthly payment alone, so the balance after the horizon is the starting balance minus the total payments made.

How is this different from the Rent Compare Calculator?

The Rent Compare Calculator compares two rentals against each other. This calculator compares selling your own home now with renting that same home out and selling it later.
