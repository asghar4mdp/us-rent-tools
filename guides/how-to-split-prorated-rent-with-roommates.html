How to Split Prorated Rent With Roommates

Short answer: Split by days, not by the calendar month. Each roommate pays the daily rent for the days they actually lived there, then you split the shared days by your normal ratio. Splitting the whole month 50/50 when one person arrived on the 16th overcharges them for two weeks they did not live there.

The formula

  1. Daily rent = monthly rent ÷ days in the month.
  2. Days with fewer roommates: whoever was there pays the full daily rate.
  3. Days with everyone there: split the daily rate by your normal ratio (50/50, or by room size).

Worked example: roommate joins mid-month

Rent is $1,600 in a 30-day month. You moved in on the 1st; your roommate joins on the 16th.

PeriodCalculationWho pays
Days 1–15, you alone15 × ($1,600 ÷ 30) = $800You: $800
Days 16–30, both of you15 × ($1,600 ÷ 30) = $800, split evenlyYou: $400, Roommate: $400
Total$1,600You: $1,200, Roommate: $400

The common mistake is splitting the whole month 50/50 — $800 each. That charges your roommate $400 for two weeks they did not live there. Get the daily rate and partial-month total first with the Prorated Rent Calculator, then apply the day counts above.

Roommate moves out mid-month

Use the same logic in reverse. The departing roommate pays their share only up to their move-out date. The remaining roommate covers the rest of the month, unless the landlord accepts a prorated amount for the whole unit — confirm which applies in your lease, because the landlord's total is usually still the full monthly rent.

Three rules that prevent arguments

  • Agree on the method before anyone moves furniture in, and write it down.
  • Keep rent and utilities separate: rent can follow room size, but shared utilities are usually cleanest split evenly.
  • Recalculate when circumstances change — a room switch, a departure, or a new roommate — rather than letting an old split linger.

Frequently asked questions

Should a roommate who moves in mid-month pay half the month?

No, not for the days before they moved in. Split by days as above.

What if roommates pay different shares normally?

Use the same ratio only for the shared days. Do not apply it to days when only one person lived there.
