First and Last Month's Rent Explained

Short answer

First month's rent pays for your first month; last month's rent is a prepayment of your final month, held by the landlord until then. Rent is commonly paid in advance — for the month ahead — but the lease controls: if your lease says otherwise, the lease governs. Last month's rent is not the same as a security deposit, and the two must not be confused.

What first month's rent is

First month's rent is the rent for your first month of tenancy, normally paid at or before move-in. If you move in mid-month, the first payment may be prorated for the partial month — use the Prorated Rent Calculator for that figure and check which proration method the lease uses. First month's rent is rent, not a deposit: once paid, it covers that month and is not refundable as a deposit would be.

What last month's rent is

Last month's rent is an advance payment of the rent for your final month, collected at move-in and held until that month arrives. When the final month comes, it is applied to that month's rent instead of you paying again. It is not a security deposit: a deposit covers damage or unpaid amounts and is returned (less lawful deductions) after move-out, while last month's rent is simply rent paid early. The distinction matters for deadlines and deductions — deposit rules, such as California's 21-day return period under Civil Code 1950.5 or Texas's 30-day period after the forwarding-address trigger, govern deposits, not prepaid rent, and this guide asserts no equivalent deadline for last month's rent.

Worked example: what is due at move-in

So, what does first and last month's rent mean in cash terms? At $1,800 per month rent, first month $1,800 + last month $1,800 = $3,600 due at move-in for rent alone, before any deposit or fees. That $3,600 figure is rent only: a deposit, if one is required, is extra. Warning: whether last month's rent is prepaid at all depends on the lease and local rules — check the official source for your city and state before assuming it is required, or that it is not.

Do you pay rent in advance or arrears?

Commonly in advance: rent due on the first of the month pays for that month ahead, not the month just ended. But this is a convention, not a universal law, and the lease controls. Some leases state a different due date or an arrears arrangement, and a lease term that is lawful in your area can still change the timing. Before you pay, check three things in the lease: the due date, which month each payment covers, and whether any payment is described as first month, last month, deposit, or a fee — because each label carries different consequences at move-out.

Move-in cash, and move-out checks

  • At move-in, add first month, last month (if required), deposit, and fees together — the Move-in Cost Calculator totals them — so the cash requirement is not a surprise.
  • Keep proof of the last month's payment and the lease clause that describes it; at move-out, confirm in writing that it will be applied to the final month.
  • Do not assume last month's rent can be used as a deposit, or a deposit as last month's rent, unless the lease or applicable law clearly allows it — check the official source for your city and state.
  • If rent increases during the tenancy, check whether the prepaid last month's amount is topped up to the new rent or remains at the old figure — the lease should say.

Frequently asked questions

What does first and last month's rent mean?

First month's rent pays for the first month; last month's rent prepays the final month and is applied when that month arrives.

Do you pay rent for the month ahead or behind?

Commonly in advance, for the month ahead — but the lease controls, so check the due date and which month each payment covers.

Is last month's rent the same as a security deposit?

No. Last month's rent is prepaid rent; a deposit is held against damage or unpaid amounts and returned under deposit rules after move-out.

Can you pay rent early?

A lease normally allows early payment, and last month's rent is itself an early payment. Check the lease for any term on early or advance payments.
