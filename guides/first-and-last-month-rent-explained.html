First and Last Month's Rent Explained

Short answer

First month's rent pays for the first month. Last month's rent prepays the final month and is applied when that month arrives. Rent is commonly paid in advance, for the month ahead, but the lease controls the due date and what each payment covers. Last month's rent is prepaid rent, not a security deposit.

This is general information, not legal advice. City ordinances may be stricter and may override state rules. Check the official source for your city and state before acting.

What first month's rent is

First month's rent is the rent for your first month of tenancy, normally paid at or before move-in. If you move in mid-month, the first payment may be prorated for the partial month — use the Prorated Rent Calculator for that figure and check which proration method the lease uses. First month's rent is rent, not a deposit: once paid, it covers that month and is not refundable as a deposit would be.

What last month's rent is

Last month's rent is an advance payment of the rent for your final month, collected at move-in and held until that month arrives. When the final month comes, it is applied to that month's rent instead of you paying again. It is not a security deposit: a deposit covers damage or unpaid amounts and is returned (less lawful deductions) after move-out, while last month's rent is simply rent paid early. The distinction matters for deadlines and deductions — deposit rules, such as California's 21-day return period under Civil Code 1950.5 or Texas's 30-day period after the forwarding-address trigger, govern deposits, not prepaid rent, and this guide asserts no equivalent deadline for last month's rent.

Worked example: what is due at move-in

So, what does first and last month's rent mean in cash terms? At $1,800 per month rent, first month $1,800 + last month $1,800 = $3,600 due at move-in for rent alone, before any deposit or fees. That $3,600 figure is rent only: a deposit, if one is required, is extra. Warning: whether last month's rent is prepaid at all depends on the lease and local rules — check the official source for your city and state before assuming it is required, or that it is not.

Do you pay rent in advance or arrears?

Commonly in advance: rent due on the first of the month pays for that month ahead, not the month just ended. But this is a convention, not a universal law, and the lease controls. Some leases state a different due date or an arrears arrangement, and a lease term that is lawful in your area can still change the timing. Before you pay, check three things in the lease: the due date, which month each payment covers, and whether any payment is described as first month, last month, deposit, or a fee — because each label carries different consequences at move-out.

Move-in cash, and move-out checks

  • At move-in, add first month, last month (if required), deposit, and fees together — the Move-in Cost Calculator totals them — so the cash requirement is not a surprise.
  • Keep proof of the last month's payment and the lease clause that describes it; at move-out, confirm in writing that it will be applied to the final month.
  • Do not assume last month's rent can be used as a deposit, or a deposit as last month's rent, unless the lease or applicable law clearly allows it — check the official source for your city and state.
  • If rent increases during the tenancy, check whether the prepaid last month's amount is topped up to the new rent or remains at the old figure — the lease should say.

How the payment calendar works

Under the common advance-payment pattern, the calendar is simple once it is written out. Rent for January is due on 1 January (or on the due date the lease states), rent for February on 1 February, and so on. Each payment buys the month that follows it, not the month just ended. Last month's rent fits into that calendar as a payment made at the start that is held until the final month: it is not an extra month's rent on top of the lease total, it is the final month's rent paid early. Over a 12-month lease at $1,800, the tenant pays $21,600 in rent in total, whether last month's rent is prepaid or not; prepayment changes when the final $1,800 is paid, not how much rent is paid overall.

Worked calendar for a 12-month lease starting 1 March at $1,800 a month, with last month's rent prepaid: at move-in, first month $1,800 plus last month $1,800 = $3,600 for rent. From April through January, the tenant pays $1,800 at the start of each month for that month — ten payments covering April to January. In February, the final month, no new payment is due for rent, because the prepaid $1,800 is applied. Counting the months confirms the total: March (first month, paid at move-in) plus April through January (ten months) plus February (prepaid) = twelve months, and $1,800 × 12 = $21,600. The move-in $3,600 plus ten monthly payments of $1,800 ($18,000) equals $21,600. Counting months this way, on paper, before signing, catches the common error of paying the final month twice.

Mid-month move-ins and prorated first months

When a tenancy starts mid-month, the first payment is often prorated for the partial month rather than charged as a full month. Example: rent $1,800 a month, move-in on 16 March in a 31-day month. The tenant occupies 16 days (16 to 31 March inclusive, depending on how the lease counts days — check the lease's counting method). On a simple daily-rate method, $1,800 ÷ 31 = $58.06 a day, and 16 days gives $929.03. On a 30-day method, $1,800 ÷ 30 = $60.00 a day, and 16 days gives $960.00. The two methods differ by $30.97 on this single payment, so the lease's proration method matters and should be stated before payment. The Prorated Rent Calculator shows both methods for comparison.

Advance versus arrears, and what the lease labels mean

The label in the lease is what counts, not the convention — and three lease phrases in particular should never be treated as interchangeable. “First month's rent” is rent for the first month. “Last month's rent” is rent for the final month, paid early. “Security deposit” is money held against damage or unpaid amounts, returned after move-out subject to lawful deductions and to the deposit rules that apply — on this site, California's 21-day return period under Civil Code 1950.5 and Texas's 30-day period after the tenant provides a written forwarding address are the scoped examples, and other places must be checked against the official source. A payment labeled as a fee is none of those three: it is a charge for a service or for processing, and it is not normally applied to rent or returned as a deposit.

Rent increases and the prepaid last month

If rent rises during the tenancy, the prepaid last month's amount may no longer equal the final month's rent. Example: last month's rent prepaid at $1,800, then rent rises to $1,950 for the remaining months. When the final month arrives at $1,950, the prepaid $1,800 covers part of it, leaving $150 to pay, unless the lease states that the prepaid amount is topped up at the time of the increase or that it remains at the original figure and the difference is waived. The lease should state which outcome applies, and if it does not, the point should be clarified in writing before the increase takes effect, not discovered in the final month. For any rent-increase rule that may apply in your state or city, check the official source, and see the Rent Rules Explained pillar guide for the increase caps scoped on this site.

Edge case: ending the lease early

If a tenancy ends early, the treatment of prepaid last month's rent depends on the lease and on applicable law, and this guide asserts no universal rule for it. In one pattern, the prepaid amount is applied to the last month actually occupied, and any remaining rent or charges are settled separately. In another, the lease may treat early termination as a breach with its own charges, and the prepaid amount may be applied against amounts owed under those terms. The tenant's protection in either pattern is the same: the lease text, the payment receipts, and a written move-out statement. Do not withhold a payment on the assumption that prepaid rent will cover it, and do not assume a prepaid amount will be refunded as if it were a deposit, without checking the lease and the official source for your area.

Frequently asked questions

What does first and last month's rent mean?

First month's rent pays for the first month; last month's rent prepays the final month and is applied when that month arrives.

Do you pay rent for the month ahead or behind?

Commonly in advance, for the month ahead — but the lease controls, so check the due date and which month each payment covers.

Is last month's rent the same as a security deposit?

No. Last month's rent is prepaid rent; a deposit is held against damage or unpaid amounts and returned under deposit rules after move-out.

Can you pay rent early?

A lease normally allows early payment, and last month's rent is itself an early payment. Check the lease for any term on early or advance payments.

Do I pay first and last month's rent plus a deposit?

Possibly, if the lease requires all three. At $1,800 a month with a deposit equal to one month, that is $5,400 at move-in for rent and deposit combined, before fees. Whether each item is required depends on the lease and on applicable law, so total your own combination with the Move-in Cost Calculator before move-in day.

What happens to last month's rent if rent goes up?

The prepaid amount may fall short of the final month's higher rent, leaving a top-up to pay, unless the lease states otherwise. Check the lease's increase and prepayment clauses, and clarify the top-up in writing when the increase takes effect.

Can last month's rent be used to pay for damage?

No, not as a general rule: last month's rent is rent, and damage is normally addressed through the deposit, subject to deposit rules. Do not assume one pot can be used for the other's purpose unless the lease or applicable law clearly allows it — check the official source for your area.

What if I move in mid-month — is last month's rent still a full month?

That depends on the lease. The first month may be prorated while last month's rent is collected in full, or both may be prorated. Get the proration method and the last-month treatment in writing, and check the figures with the Prorated Rent Calculator.

Do I pay rent in the final month if I prepaid last month's rent?

No, not for that month's rent itself, if the prepaid amount equals the final month's rent and the lease applies it to that month. If rent has risen, or the final month is partial, a difference may remain, so confirm the final-month statement in writing before that month begins.

Same warning as above: this is general information, not legal advice — check the official source for your city and state.
