Prorated Rent When Breaking a Lease Early

Short answer: Prorated rent is only one part of an early break. Separate it into four buckets: (1) rent for days occupied in the final month, (2) rent for any notice period you still owe, (3) an early termination fee if your lease states one, and (4) any unused prepaid rent that might be refundable. Adding them together — or assuming the prorated figure is the whole bill — is the most common mistake.

Step 1: Prorate the final month

Daily rent = monthly rent ÷ days in that month. Multiply by days occupied, counting the move-out date as occupied on this site unless your lease says otherwise. Use the Prorated Rent Calculator to compare actual-days, 30-day, and 365-day figures against your lease method.

Step 2: Check the notice period

If your lease or state law requires, say, 30 days' notice, rent for that notice period may still be due even if you leave sooner — unless you have a signed early-termination agreement. Get that agreement in writing, with the final date, the final amount, and what happens to the deposit.

Worked example: unused prepaid rent

Monthly rent $1,800 in a 30-day month. You prepaid the full month, then move out after 24 days, with no notice-period rent left and no termination fee in the lease.

ItemCalculationAmount
Daily rent$1,800 ÷ 30$60.00
Days occupied24 days$1,440.00
Unused prepaid days6 days × $60$360.00

That $360 is the mathematical unused amount before any lease terms, fees, or deductions. It is not automatically a refund — your lease, termination agreement, and applicable law decide whether it is returned, applied to other amounts owed, or forfeited.

What to do before you hand back the keys

  • Read the early-termination, notice, and holdover clauses in your lease first.
  • Put any agreed early exit in writing, with dates and amounts.
  • Photograph the unit, keep the forwarding address in writing, and track the deposit return deadline separately — deposit rules are not the same as prorated rent rules.
  • Do not stop paying rent on the assumption that the deposit will cover it, unless you have written agreement to do so.

Frequently asked questions

Do I only owe prorated rent if I break my lease mid-month?

Not necessarily. Notice-period rent, termination fees, and unpaid rent can still apply.

Can I get a refund for prepaid days after I move out?

Only if your lease, a mutual termination agreement, or applicable law provides for it. Calculate the unused days first, then check the wording.
