Oregon Late Fee: ORS 90.260, the 4-Day Grace Period, and the 5% Structure

Short answer

Oregon late fee (ORS 90.260): a 4-day grace period, the fee must be in a written rental agreement, and the structure is 5% of periodic rent for each succeeding 5-day period — as one statutory structure, not a single flat 5% overall cap. Example: $2,000 rent paid 11 days after the due date = $200.

Oregon tenants and landlords often misunderstand the state’s late-fee rule because it is quoted in shorthand as “5%.” The full rule under ORS 90.260 is more specific: a 4-day grace period, a requirement that the fee be in a written rental agreement, and a structure of 5% per succeeding 5-day period as one statutory structure. This guide explains each element, with examples, so you can check a fee rather than guess at it.

The 4-day grace period

Under ORS 90.260, rent paid within 4 days after the due date does not attract a late fee under this rule. If rent is due on the 1st, payment on the 2nd, 3rd, 4th, or 5th is within the grace period; payment on the 6th is outside it. Count carefully from the due date stated in your agreement, and keep proof of the actual payment date — a bank record or a landlord receipt — because that date, not the date you meant to pay, determines whether the grace period was met. A grace period is not permission to pay late every month: arrears can still have other consequences under the tenancy law, even when no fee is due for a particular month.

The fee must be in a written agreement

A late fee in Oregon must be set out in a written rental agreement before it can be charged. A verbal understanding, a notice posted after the tenancy began, or a fee added to a bill without an agreement term is not a substitute. Before paying a fee, read your written agreement’s late-fee clause: does it state the fee, how it is calculated, and when it applies? If there is no such clause, raise that in writing before paying. Landlords should ensure the clause is in the signed agreement from the start, not added later without proper agreement.

How the 5% per succeeding 5-day period works

ORS 90.260 uses 5% per succeeding 5-day period as one statutory structure. In practice, that means the fee is assessed in 5-day blocks after the grace period, rather than as one flat 5% charge for any lateness. For example, on $1,800 rent, one 5-day period at 5% is $90. If rent remains unpaid into a second succeeding 5-day period, a further period charge may apply under the agreement and statute as written — which is why the total can exceed a single 5% figure for longer delays. Do not present or accept this as a flat 5% overall cap: the per-period structure, the agreement’s wording, and the number of periods elapsed all matter. Check the agreement and the official source together for your exact case.

Worked examples

Example 1: rent due 1 October, paid 4 October. Within the 4-day grace period — no late fee under ORS 90.260. Example 2: rent due 1 October, paid 8 October. Outside the grace period, in the first succeeding 5-day period — one period charge applies under the agreement’s structure, $90 on $1,800 rent at 5%. Example 3: rent due 1 October, still unpaid on 15 October. More than one succeeding 5-day period has elapsed, so the agreement’s per-period calculation must be applied period by period, not as a single $90 charge. Always write the due date, payment date, grace days, and periods elapsed next to the fee figure so either side can check it.

If you disagree with a fee

  • Write down the due date, payment date, grace period, and periods elapsed.
  • Check the written agreement for the fee clause and its calculation.
  • Ask the landlord in writing to show the calculation period by period.
  • If the fee was charged inside the grace period or without a written term, say so in writing with the dates.
  • Seek local housing advice if the fee is large, repeated, or combined with a notice to vacate.

Frequently asked questions

What is Oregon’s grace period?

4 days after the due date, under ORS 90.260.

Must the fee be written down?

Yes. In Oregon the late fee must be in a written rental agreement.

Is the fee capped at 5% total?

No — not as a flat overall cap. The statute uses 5% per succeeding 5-day period as one structure, so longer delays can involve more than one period charge.
