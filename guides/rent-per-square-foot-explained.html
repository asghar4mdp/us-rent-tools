Rent per Square Foot Explained

Short answer

Rent per square foot is monthly rent divided by area, and it lets you compare homes of different sizes fairly. Example: $2,000 ÷ 800 sq ft = $2.50 per sq ft per month; at that rate, a 1,000 sq ft home would cost $2,500. The figure is only trustworthy if every home's area was measured the same way — check that before you compare.

What it is

Rent per square foot answers a simple question: for each square foot of home, how many dollars of monthly rent are you paying? The formula is monthly rent ÷ area in square feet. A $2,000 home at 800 sq ft costs $2.50 per sq ft; a $2,500 home at 1,250 sq ft costs $2.00 per sq ft — the more expensive home is actually cheaper per square foot. That is the whole point: headline rent rewards small homes, while rent per square foot rewards space.

Both directions

To calculate rent by square footage, divide monthly rent by the area in square feet. The calculation runs both ways, and the Rent per Square Foot Calculator does both. From rent and area to a rate: $2,000 ÷ 800 = $2.50 per sq ft. From a rate and area to a rent: $2.50 × 1,000 = $2,500 per month. The reverse direction is useful when a landlord or agent quotes a rate rather than a rent, or when you want to estimate what a home of a given size should cost at the going rate for an area.

How to compare fairly

  • Compare homes in the same area and building type — a downtown rate and a suburban rate are not competing figures.
  • Use the same area basis for every home: usable living area, gross area, and area including balconies or shared space give different rates for the same home.
  • Check what the rent includes: a rate that bundles utilities, parking, or amenities is not comparable with a bare-rent rate.
  • Pair the rate with a full cost comparison — the Rent Compare Calculator adds fees, utilities, and parking to the headline rent.

What it does not tell you

Rent per square foot says nothing about layout, light, condition, noise, or location within a building — a badly laid-out 1,000 sq ft can feel smaller than a well laid-out 800 sq ft. It also says nothing about affordability: a low rate on a very large home can still be unaffordable in total. Use it as a value check alongside total cost and affordability, not as a single verdict. Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate rent per square foot?

Divide monthly rent by area in square feet: rent ÷ sq ft = dollars per sq ft per month.

What is a good rent per square foot?

There is no universal good figure — it varies by city, neighbourhood, and building type. Compare within the same area.

Why do two listings for the same home show different rates?

Usually because the area was measured differently, or because one figure includes items the other excludes. Check the measurement basis and what the rent includes.
