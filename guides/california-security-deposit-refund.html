California Security Deposit Refund: AB 12, the 1-Month Limit, and the 21-Day Return

Short answer

California (AB 12, Civil Code 1950.5): a security deposit is generally limited to 1 month's rent, with a 2-month exception for small landlords as defined in the statute, and must generally be returned within 21 calendar days after vacating, with an itemised statement for deductions. City ordinances may be stricter.

California tenants often ask two questions at move-out: was my deposit lawful in the first place, and when must it come back? This guide answers both under AB 12 and Civil Code 1950.5, the verified California rules used on this site, and explains the practical steps that protect a refund. It is general information, not legal advice, and local ordinances may be stricter — check the official source for your city as well as the state statute.

How much can a landlord charge in California?

Under AB 12 (Civil Code 1950.5), a security deposit in California is generally limited to one month’s rent. There is a two-month exception for small landlords as defined in the statute, so a two-month deposit should not automatically be treated as unlawful — the question is whether that landlord meets the statutory definition of a small landlord. If you were charged two months, ask the landlord in writing which provision they rely on, and check the definition in the current statute. A city ordinance may impose a stricter limit, and any amount charged above the lawful limit should be queried promptly, ideally before you sign or soon after, with the lease and payment records to hand.

The 21-calendar-day return deadline

After you vacate, the landlord generally has 21 calendar days to return the deposit or the balance after lawful deductions, together with an itemised statement of deductions. Calendar days include weekends and public holidays, so count 21 days from the day after vacating as the statute directs — if you vacate on 1 October, the deadline is 22 October. Put that date in your diary at move-out. If the deadline passes with no refund and no itemised statement, write to the landlord with your move-out date, your forwarding address, and a request for the refund or statement, and keep a copy.

What can be deducted?

Lawful deductions generally cover unpaid rent and damage beyond normal wear and tear. Normal wear and tear — faded paint, light carpet wear in walkways, minor scuffs from ordinary use — should not be deducted. Damage such as a broken fixture, a large burn mark, or an unauthorised alteration may be. The itemised statement should let you see each deduction, its amount, and what it relates to. Compare it with your move-in checklist and photographs: any item that was already damaged at move-in, or that is ordinary wear, is worth challenging in writing with your evidence attached.

Move-in and move-out evidence

  • Photograph every room at move-in and move-out, with dates, including appliances, walls, floors, and windows.
  • Fill in the move-in checklist in detail and keep a signed copy; a blank checklist helps no one at move-out.
  • Keep the lease, rent receipts, and any repair or cleaning receipts.
  • Give a forwarding address in writing and keep proof of sending.
  • Leave the home clean and in the condition the lease requires, and return all keys as directed.

If your refund is late or short

First, check your dates and your evidence, then write to the landlord setting out the deadline, the amount you expect, and the items you dispute. If the landlord does not respond, consider contacting a local housing department, a tenant organisation, or a qualified professional — California law provides remedies for certain failures to return a deposit on time, but the procedure and any penalty depend on the facts and the current statute, so do not assume a particular penalty applies without checking the official source. Keep every letter and email; a clear paper trail is the strongest asset in a deposit dispute.

What is California’s deposit limit?

How long does a landlord have to return a deposit in California?

Generally 21 calendar days after the tenant vacates, with an itemised statement for any deductions.
