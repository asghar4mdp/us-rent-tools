Texas Security Deposit Deadline: 30 Days and the Forwarding-Address Trigger

Short answer

Texas: a security deposit must generally be returned within 30 days (Property Code 92.103, 92.107), and the tenant's written forwarding address is the trigger that starts the landlord's duty. Texas has no statutory cap on the deposit amount; the lease amount controls, subject to any local ordinance.

Texas does not cap the amount of a security deposit by statute, but it does set a clear return deadline — and that deadline turns on the tenant’s forwarding address. This guide explains the 30-day rule under Property Code 92.103 and 92.107, why giving a forwarding address in writing matters so much, and what to do if the deadline passes.

The 30-day rule

Under Texas Property Code 92.103 and 92.107, a landlord must generally return the deposit, or the balance after lawful deductions with an itemised description, within 30 days. The precise trigger for that 30-day period is tied to the tenant’s move-out and the forwarding address as the statute describes it, so the safest approach is to give the forwarding address in writing at or immediately after move-out and to count 30 days from the trigger the statute sets out, keeping proof of both dates. If you move out on 1 October and the trigger is met that day, the 30-day deadline would fall on 31 October. Mark it on your calendar; do not rely on memory weeks later.

Why the forwarding address is the trigger

A landlord cannot return a deposit to an address they do not have. Texas law therefore makes the tenant’s forwarding address a central step: without it, the landlord’s obligation to send the refund or statement operates differently, and a tenant who complains about a late refund without ever giving an address is in a much weaker position. Give the address in writing — by email, letter, or a message through a channel you can prove — and keep a copy with the date. A verbal mention to a caretaker or a casual remark is not a safe substitute. If you move again within the 30 days, update the address in writing straight away.

No statutory cap, but deductions still matter

Because Texas has no statutory cap on the deposit amount, the amount itself is set by the lease and the market. That does not mean any deduction is lawful. Deductions should still relate to unpaid rent or damage beyond normal wear and tear, and the landlord should provide an itemised description of deductions. Compare each item with your move-in photographs and checklist, and challenge anything that is pre-existing damage or ordinary wear. Keep receipts for cleaning or repairs you carried out yourself.

If the deadline passes

  • Recheck the trigger date: move-out date and the date you gave the forwarding address in writing.
  • Write to the landlord with both dates, the 30-day deadline, and a request for the refund or itemised statement.
  • Keep the lease, move-in and move-out photos, forwarding-address proof, and all correspondence.
  • If there is no response, contact a local housing organisation or seek qualified advice. Texas law provides for certain consequences when a landlord fails to return a deposit in good faith by the deadline, but the remedy and procedure are fact-specific — check the official source before asserting a particular amount.
  • Do not deduct the deposit from rent in a new tenancy or take self-help steps without advice.

Move-out checklist for Texas tenants

Clean to the lease standard, photograph every room, return keys as directed, and hand over or send your forwarding address in writing on the same day if you can. Those four steps, done on move-out day, protect more deposit money than any argument made weeks later. Landlords, for their part, should diarise the 30-day deadline at move-out, prepare the itemised statement promptly, and send the refund to the forwarding address by a provable method.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a Texas landlord have to return a deposit?

Generally 30 days, under Property Code 92.103 and 92.107, with the forwarding-address trigger described in the statute.

What if I never gave a forwarding address?

The landlord’s obligations operate differently without one. Give it in writing now and keep proof, then seek advice on your position.

Is there a cap on a Texas deposit?

No statutory cap. The amount is set by the lease, subject to any local ordinance.
