California Landlord Missed the 21-Day Deposit Deadline: What Happens

Short answer: Under California Civil Code §1950.5, a landlord has 21 calendar days from the date you vacate — not the lease end date — to return the deposit or send an itemised statement with the remaining balance. Missing that deadline can mean losing the right to keep deductions, and if a court finds bad faith, it can award up to twice the deposit on top of returning it.

The two tiers of consequence

SituationPossible outcome
Late, but not bad faithCourts frequently disallow deductions when the 21-day requirements are not met, especially if the tenant was prejudiced by the delay. Even legitimate damage claims can be lost on timing alone.
Late and bad faithA court can order return of the deposit plus up to twice the wrongfully withheld amount, plus actual damages, under §1950.5(l). Bad faith can include no documentation, no attempt to itemise, or ignoring the deadline entirely.

Worked example

Deposit $2,800, no itemised statement within 21 days, and a court finds bad faith. The tenant could recover the $2,800 deposit plus up to $5,600 in bad-faith damages — $8,400 before any actual damages or costs the court allows. That is why a one-day miss is not a small administrative slip in California.

AB 2801: the photo rule landlords miss

Under AB 2801, effective July 1, 2025, deductions generally require both move-in and move-out photo documentation. Without move-in photos, a landlord may not be able to prove damage occurred during the tenancy, regardless of the actual condition. Tenants should keep their own dated move-in and move-out photos as well.

What to do if your deadline has passed

  1. Calculate the deadline from your actual move-out and possession-return date with the Security Deposit Calculator.
  2. Send a dated written demand citing §1950.5, by certified mail and email, keeping copies.
  3. Keep the lease, move-in/move-out photos, forwarding address proof, and any landlord messages.
  4. If it is not resolved, California small claims court handles these disputes regularly — but get qualified advice if the amount is large or a dispute is already underway.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if a California landlord misses the 21-day deadline?

Deductions can be lost, and bad faith can add up to twice the deposit on top of return.

Do landlords need photos to deduct from a California deposit?

Under AB 2801, deductions generally require both move-in and move-out photo documentation.
