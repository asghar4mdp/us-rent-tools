Getting a deposit back is often slower and more disputed than paying it. The deadline for return, what counts as return, and what a landlord must send with it all vary by state. This guide explains the verified deadlines, the steps that start the clock, and what to do if the deadline passes without a refund or an itemised statement.

California: 21 calendar days

In California, under Civil Code 1950.5 (as amended by AB 12), a landlord generally must return the deposit — or the balance after lawful deductions, with an itemised statement — within 21 calendar days of the tenant vacating. Calendar days include weekends and holidays, so day 21 is not extended merely because it falls on a weekend unless the statute or a court rule says otherwise for your situation. If you vacate on 1 October, day 21 is 22 October. Mark the date on your calendar at move-out, not when you remember to check weeks later.

Texas: 30 days and the forwarding-address trigger

In Texas, the deposit must generally be returned within 30 days, under Property Code 92.103 and 92.107, and the tenant’s forwarding address plays a triggering role. In practical terms, give your forwarding address in writing, keep proof that you sent it and when, and count 30 days from the trigger the statute describes. A tenant who never gives a forwarding address may find the deadline and the landlord’s obligations operate differently, so this single step — a short written notice with your new address — is one of the most important things you can do at move-out. Do not rely on a verbal forwarding address or on the landlord already knowing where you moved.

Other states: check the official source

For states without a verified rule on this site, do not borrow California’s 21 days or Texas’s 30 days. Deadlines elsewhere range widely, and some states require an itemised statement by a different date than the refund itself, or impose a penalty for late return. The official source for your state — usually the state statute or the housing department’s tenant guide — is the only safe basis for a deadline. A city ordinance may also set its own deadline or procedure.

What should you receive?

You should normally receive either the full deposit or the balance plus an itemised statement explaining each deduction, with amounts. Vague entries such as “cleaning — $400” without detail are harder to challenge and harder to justify; ask for specifics, receipts, or invoices where appropriate. Compare each deduction with your move-in photographs and checklist. Normal wear and tear should not be charged, and pre-existing damage recorded at move-in should not be charged again at move-out.

If the deadline passes

  • Check your dates: move-out date, forwarding-address date, and the deadline you calculated from the official source.
  • Write to the landlord, citing the dates and asking for the refund or itemised statement.
  • Keep copies of everything: lease, move-in and move-out photos, forwarding-address proof, and all correspondence.
  • If the landlord does not respond, contact a local housing organisation or seek qualified advice — some states provide for penalties or for a tenant to recover amounts, but the procedure is state-specific.
  • Do not withhold rent from a future tenancy or take other self-help steps without advice; they can create a separate dispute.

Prevention is easier than recovery: photograph at move-in and move-out, give a forwarding address in writing, and leave the home in the condition the lease requires. Those three habits resolve most deposit disputes before they start.

Frequently asked questions

Does the deadline include weekends?

In California the period is 21 calendar days, which includes weekends. Other states may count differently — check the official source.

What if I did not give a forwarding address?

In Texas the forwarding address triggers key obligations. Give it in writing as soon as you move, and keep proof.

What if only part of the deposit is returned?

You should receive an itemised statement for the withheld part. Challenge any item that does not match your move-in evidence.
